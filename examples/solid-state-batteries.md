# Solid-State Batteries - Technology Brief  
## What it is  
Solid-state batteries use solid electrolytes instead of liquid ones, offering higher energy density, improved safety, and wider operating temperature ranges. They are seen as a next-generation energy storage solution with potential to surpass current lithium-ion batteries [1][2][5].  

## Maturity  
Estimated TRL 6–7. Research has advanced to prototype and benchmark stages with growing industry involvement, though widespread commercial deployment remains limited. Publication data shows rapid growth in academic output, but commercial production use is underrepresented in the literature [2][6].

**Companies**: Nissan (Japan) (1), Samsung Semiconductor (United States) (1), BASF (Germany) (1)  

## Trends  
Publication counts have surged from 1,798 in 2020 to 5,661 in 2025, indicating accelerating research activity. Focus areas include interface mechanics, solid electrolyte materials (e.g., antiperovskites), silicon anodes, and cathode design [1][3][7][9]. Industry participation remains low, with only 6% of top papers including industry authors (OpenAlex).  

## Applications and open challenges  
Applications include electric vehicles and portable electronics, where safety and energy density are critical. Open challenges include poor electrochemical performance of silicon anodes, degradation at solid-solid interfaces, and insufficient understanding of chemo-mechanical failure mechanisms [11]. Despite high ionic conductivity in sulfide, oxide, and halide electrolytes, long-term stability and scalability remain unresolved [3].
