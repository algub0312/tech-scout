# Software Supply Chain Security - Technology Brief  
## What it is  
Software supply chain security refers to protecting the entire lifecycle of software—from development and dependency management to deployment—against malicious attacks, such as those exploiting vulnerabilities in open-source libraries or third-party tools [1]. These attacks have become increasingly common, as seen in incidents like SolarWinds and log4j [1].  

## Maturity  
Estimated TRL 5–6. Research shows growing industry involvement in prototypes and benchmarks, with increasing focus on end-to-end security models and secure design properties [4]. However, publication data underrepresents commercial deployment, and no paper explicitly reports widespread production use, so commercial adoption remains limited [n].

## Trends  
Research output has grown exponentially, from 39 papers in 2020 to 766 in 2026 (partial year), indicating rising academic interest [OpenAlex]. Industry participation is present but limited, with only 18% of top papers having at least one industry author [OpenAlex]. Emerging trends include AI-driven threats, quantum risks, and integration of security into DevSecOps and CI/CD pipelines [12][10].  

## Applications and open challenges  
Applications span cloud-native systems, web3 dApps, and API-based services, where dependency complexity and immutability introduce unique risks [6][7]. Key challenges include managing transitive dependencies, ensuring transparency and validity in supply chains, and aligning security with agile development practices [4][3]. The lack of standardized, widely adopted frameworks and limited real-world deployment data remain significant gaps [11].
