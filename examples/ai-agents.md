# AI Agents - Technology Brief  
## What it is  
AI Agents are autonomous systems capable of pursuing complex goals with minimal human supervision, using reasoning, planning, and tool integration to perform tasks across domains [3][6]. They differ from standard AI models by enabling self-directed workflows, task execution, and multi-step decision-making [3].  

## Maturity  
Estimated TRL 5–6. Publication growth reflects rapid academic progress and prototype development, with increasing industry involvement in benchmarks and use cases, though commercial deployment remains underrepresented in publications [OpenAlex].  

## Key players  
**Academic institutions**: Harvard University, University of Cambridge, University of Oxford, Centre for the Governance of AI, University of Toronto, Sungkyunkwan University  
**Companies**: Microsoft (United States), Infosys (India), Capgemini (Netherlands), Capgemini (United States), Ant Group (China), Huawei Technologies (China), Microsoft Research Asia (China)  

## Trends  
Publication counts have surged from 842 in 2020 to 34,464 in 2026 (partial), indicating exponential growth in research activity [OpenAlex]. The distinction between AI Agents and Agentic AI is being formalized, emphasizing modularity and task-specific automation [3][6]. Industry authorship in top papers accounts for only 14%, suggesting limited commercial integration despite academic momentum [OpenAlex].  

## Applications and open challenges  
Applications span biomedical discovery [1], clinical medicine [11], customer service [4], and creative design [12]. AI agents are being tested in healthcare for task planning and multi-omic analysis [9][11], while emotional expression in customer service is studied for its impact on user trust [4][10]. Key challenges include security vulnerabilities [7], ethical risks from autonomous decision-making [2], and difficulties in human-AI collaboration in creative processes [12].
